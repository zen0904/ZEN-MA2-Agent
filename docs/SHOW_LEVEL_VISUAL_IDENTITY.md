# Show-Level Visual Identity

## Purpose

ZEN should not design a multi-song show as one repeated song template with
different colors, nor force every song to be unrelated to every other song.

The target is:

```text
SHOW LANGUAGE != SONG TEMPLATE
SONG IDENTITY != FORCED NOVELTY
```

A coherent show may deliberately reuse visual ideas, palette families, spatial
relationships, silhouettes, or movement vocabulary. A good song treatment may
also deliberately break from the surrounding language. The design task is to
make those relationships intentional and traceable.

## Core distinction

### Show language

The recurring visual grammar that makes the production feel like one show.

Examples can include:

- hierarchy of performer / band / scenic / audience;
- recurring spatial or architectural relationships;
- a restrained palette family;
- a characteristic use of silhouette or negative space;
- recurring movement vocabulary;
- repeated visual motifs or callbacks;
- a recognizable balance between restraint and scale.

Show language is not a requirement that every song use the same look.

### Song identity

The visual reason a song belongs in this show and still feels like itself.

Song identity may come from one or more of:

- focus and hierarchy;
- palette behavior, including keeping a palette static;
- density and negative space;
- movement character;
- timing and punctuation;
- texture;
- performer or choreography emphasis;
- scale;
- use or deliberate non-use of a mechanism;
- relationship to a lyric, narrative, video or scenic idea.

Changing hue alone does not establish song identity.

## Relationship types

These are reasoning labels, not mandatory schema values.

### CONTINUE

The song intentionally remains inside the same visual family as its
predecessor or chapter.

Use when continuity is more valuable than novelty.

### DEVELOP

The song retains recognizable material but expands, reduces, reframes or
recombines it.

This is the normal form of "variation on a theme."

### CONTRAST

The song deliberately changes the dominant visual strategy to create a real
turn in the set.

Contrast can come from scale, focus, movement, density, palette, texture,
negative space or another justified dimension. A color swap alone is usually a
weak form of contrast unless color itself is the meaningful event.

### CALLBACK

A previously established visual motif returns later with intentional meaning.

A callback should be recognizable but may change role, scale, context or
implementation.

### RESET

The visual field is deliberately simplified or cleared to create a new
starting point.

A reset can be useful after a dense chapter or before a major change, but
"bridge goes dark" must not become a template.

## Anti-template reasoning

Before finalizing a song treatment, compare it against nearby and earlier
songs.

Ask:

- Is this actually a new design decision, or the previous song with different
  parameter values?
- Is the same verse / pre-chorus / chorus / bridge formula being repeated
  without musical or narrative justification?
- Is every chorus made "bigger" in the same way?
- Is every bridge solved with blackout, blue, silhouette, or no movement?
- Is every final chorus solved by turning on everything?
- Is the same fixture family always assigned the same artistic role?
- Is a repeated look functioning as a motif, or merely as programming reuse?
- Is the proposed difference serving the song, or only satisfying a desire to
  be different?
- Would intentional similarity make the set stronger here?
- Has a strong earlier idea earned a callback later?
- Does the song have at least one meaningful identity dimension beyond hue?

The Critic should identify rubber-stamp behavior explicitly, but should also
reject forced novelty when variation damages show coherence.

## Visual fingerprint

For reasoning and review, a song can be described with a qualitative visual
fingerprint such as:

```text
dominant hierarchy
palette behavior
movement character
density / negative space
scale
texture
focus / position strategy
timing / punctuation strategy
withheld mechanisms
relationship to previous song
relationship to show-level motifs
```

This is not a numeric diversity score.

ZEN must not optimize for maximum distance between fingerprints. Similarity can
be correct when it represents a chapter, motif, callback or coherent show
language.

## Color-only work

When only color and dimmer are available, ZEN must not pretend that changing
hue creates a fully distinct design.

Color-only identity can still vary through:

- monochromatic versus dual-color structure;
- neutral plus accent;
- static palette versus progressive change;
- saturation and tint development;
- deliberate absence of color;
- holding a color through structural changes;
- one hard color event;
- callback to an earlier palette;
- intensity, hierarchy and negative-space relationships around the palette.

A twelve-song color-only show may legitimately reuse a palette across multiple
songs or chapters. Reuse is not failure when the behavior, hierarchy and
reason differ. Conversely, twelve different palettes do not constitute twelve
different designs by themselves.

## Set-level review

A completed multi-song candidate should be reviewed at two levels:

```text
SONG REVIEW
Does this treatment serve this song?

SET REVIEW
Does this song contribute the right amount of continuity, development,
contrast, callback or reset within the larger show?
```

Set review should flag:

- accidental template reuse;
- mechanism overuse;
- unjustified palette churn;
- repeated structural tricks;
- lack of callbacks or chapter coherence where they would help;
- forced differentiation;
- songs whose identity exists only as a color substitution.

## Evidence boundary

Professional examples may demonstrate that designers:

- preserve a successful visual theme while varying its implementation;
- keep several songs inside a tightly restricted chapter palette;
- reserve strong color for rare high-impact moments;
- establish a visual language early and call it back later;
- create different song recipes inside one coherent aesthetic.

These are conditional practices, not universal recipes.

No external case establishes a requirement such as:

```text
every song must be unique
every chorus must differ
every song must have a new palette
every repeated section must escalate
```

The correct relationship remains dependent on the music, performance,
production concept, show position and available evidence.
